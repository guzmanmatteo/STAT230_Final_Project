Research Questions

  • Does installed renewable electricity capacity growth from 2000-2022 correlate with renewable electricity generation?

  • Is there a relationship between the amount of international funding for renewable energy given to developing countries and the growth of installed renewable electricity capacity in those countries from 2000-2022?

Data & Clean-Up

I am using 2 data sets:

One contains the amount of international financing in 2021 dollars that each developing country received from 2000-2022. This data was sourced from the website “Our World In Data” (https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/international-finance-clean-energy), who in turn sourced their data from multiple sources compiled by the UN via UN SDG Indicators Database (https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/dataportal), UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs (accessed 2024). More information available at: https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-07-0a-01.pdf. This data set contains 4 variables:

-Entity (categorical) - this is the country name

-Code (categorical) - this is the country code

-Year (numeric discrete) - this is the year

-Financing (numeric continuous) - this is the amount of money the country received from international sources. (Please note that “Financing” is not the actual variable name, a long description loaded in as the variable name when downloading from the database. I will change it to “Financing” in my code.)

The second data set contains data about the amount of electricity generated and also total installed capacity by each country each year from 2000-2023, and breaks it down by whether it was renewable or not, and if it was on-grid or off-grid. This data was sourced from the “International Renewable Energy Agency” (IRENA) from its Data web page (https://www.irena.org/Data). It collects data from its 170 member countries on renewable energy. This data set contains 6 variables:

-Country/area (categorical) - this is the country or region name

-Technology (categorical) - this is how the electricity was generated (renewable, nonrenewable, etc)

-Data Type (categorical) - this is whether the row displays the actual amount of electricity generated for the whole year, or whether it displays the amount of electrical capacity the country had that year, regardless of how much was actually generated

-Grid connection (categorical) - this is whether the data in the row is for on-grid, off-grid or both (All)

-Year (numeric discrete) - this is the year

-Electricity Statistics (MW/GWh) (numeric continuous) - this is the amount of electricity, in MW or GWh (depending on the data type variable - GWh for electricity generation, and MW for capacity)

renewable_capacity_2000 <- elecstat |>
  filter(Technology == "Total Renewable",
         `Data Type` == "Electricity Installed Capacity (MW)",
         `Grid connection` == "On-grid",
          Year == 2000) |>
  select(`Country/area`, capacity_2000 = `Electricity statistics (MW/GWh)`)

renewable_capacity_2022 <- elecstat |>
  filter(Technology == "Total Renewable",
         `Data Type` == "Electricity Installed Capacity (MW)",
         `Grid connection` == "On-grid",
          Year == 2022) |>
  select(`Country/area`, capacity_2022 = `Electricity statistics (MW/GWh)`)

combined_capacity <- renewable_capacity_2000 |>
  full_join(renewable_capacity_2022, by = "Country/area")

combined_capacity <- combined_capacity |>
  mutate(capacity_2000 = replace_na(capacity_2000, 0),
         capacity_2022 = replace_na(capacity_2022, 0))

renewable_capacity_growth <- combined_capacity |>
  mutate(Capacity_Growth = capacity_2022 - capacity_2000) |>
  select(`Country/area`, Capacity_Growth)

#create generation total data

renewable_generation_total <- elecstat |>
  filter(Technology == "Total Renewable",
         `Data Type` == "Electricity Generation (GWh)",
         `Grid connection` == "On-grid",
          Year != 2023) |>
              group_by(`Country/area`) |>
                  summarize(Total_Renewable_Generation =

There was a strong, statistically significant correlation between capacity growth and generation, with an r of 0.9593 (which is very strong as it is close to 1) and a p-value of < 2.2e-16 (which is very close to 0 and is much less than 0.05). Conclusion

The analysis of these datasets showed that there is a nearly perfect linear relationship (R-squared = 0.92) between renewable capacity growth and renewable electricity generation, which establishes that countries that built more capacity tended to produce more renewable electricity during 2000-2022. This is an important basis to establish, as it suggests that building infrastructure is crucial for increasing renewable energy production in developing countries. This clear trend can be easily visualized on the log-transformed scatter plot.

However, the international financing showed a weaker effect on renewable capacity growth. While funding did have a statistically significant effect, that effect was relatively small (only explained about 5.75% of the variance in capacity growth) and highlights that while international funding is important, other factors (such as domestic policy, geography, local corruption, or access to energy resources) could potentially be more important when considering the most effective ways to assist developing countries in transitioning towards lower-emission energy grids. This weaker relationship can be visualized in the second log-transformed scatter plots. Additionally, it is quite clear based on the large standard deviations in the summary statistics and in the various plots that a few countries both receive much more international financing and generate/install more renewable energy capacity than the majority of developing nations.

Some limitations of this analysis lie in the exclusion of some developing countries that were not present in both datasets (such as American Samoa), or that I was not able to properly match due to formatting issues (such as Turkiye or Cote’d’Ivoire/Ivory Coast). The loss of these observations, while few in number compared to the 147 observations in the final dataset, likely only had a minor effect but were still a limitation. Additionally, the countries chosen to be considered “developing” is not entirely clear, and is likely based on UN definitions of developing nations (since both original datasets were retrieved from international organizations), but those definitions are still ultimately subjective and that subjectivity has a major influence on this analysis.

Overall, while international funding is an important tool, it should be implemented alongside other measures to create a multifaceted approach, considerate of multiple factors outside of only funding (which further analyses could elucidate), to building renewable energy infrastructure in the developing world.
